The big picture: what the thesis proposes and why it matters

Chapter 1

The Core Argument

The thesis proposes a "Crypto World Bank" — not a real bank, but a research prototype showing that blockchain can mirror how international development finance actually works.

Real-world finance moves money through layers: World Bank → national banks → local banks → individuals. But today's DeFi protocols like Aave are completely flat — everyone uses the same pool, no hierarchy, no institutional roles.

Chapter 1 · Oracle Problem

Bridging Off-Chain AI to On-Chain Contracts

Smart contracts can't see the outside world. CWB's Random Forest fraud scores live off-chain — they must reach the LocalBank contract via an oracle.

  • Centralized relay — fast, but re-introduces trust (prototype fallback)
  • Chainlink Functions — DON consensus, 30–60s latency, Phase III primary
  • Commit-reveal — hash score first, reveal after decision window

§2.1
Werner et al. (2022) — ACM Systematization of Knowledge

Why DeFi Lending is Structurally "Flat"

This is the most important source in the entire thesis. Werner et al. surveyed every type of DeFi protocol and found lending platforms are uniformly pool-based and over-collateralized with no institutional hierarchy.

A billion-dollar sovereign wealth fund and an individual with a crypto wallet access the exact same pool under the same rules. There's no concept of a "national bank" or "local bank" in any existing protocol.

What is Over-Collateralization?

Aave requires 125–150% collateral — immediately excludes 1.4B unbanked adults with no digital assets

This is the structural gap the entire thesis enters.
🧠 Quick Check: Why can't an unbanked farmer use Aave?

Because Aave requires you to deposit more crypto than you want to borrow. Someone with no digital assets has nothing to deposit — they're locked out by design, not by accident.

Palaiokrassas et al. (2024) — 54M+ DeFi transactions, 23 protocols

Machine Learning for Fraud Detection

Analyzed over 54 million DeFi transactions. The crucial finding: behavioral features dramatically outperform raw transaction data.

Raw features
amount, gas paid
F1: 0.08
Behavioral features
patterns over time
F1: 0.76–0.85

Raw Transaction Feature

"Amount transferred: $500"
"Gas paid: 0.002 ETH"

Fraudsters camouflage these easily

Behavioral Feature

"Flash loan interactions in last 30 min: 7"
"Borrow-to-repay ratio this week: 0.2"

Patterns across time expose fraud

F1 Score explained: Harmonic mean of precision and recall. 0.08 = essentially random. 0.76–0.85 = real detection. CWB's Random Forest adopts behavioral feature engineering.

§2.3
Liu et al. (2008) — Isolation Forest algorithm

Isolation Forest for Anomaly Detection

CWB's secondary fraud detector — used when you don't have labeled fraud data. Instead of modeling "normal" and flagging deviations, it directly isolates anomalies.

Anomalies are few and different — they require fewer partitions to isolate. Normal points in dense clusters need many splits.

Short path length to isolate = likely anomaly (red dot far from cluster)
Labeled fraud datasets are rare in DeFi. Unusual wallet behavior — like someone suddenly borrowing across 50 wallets in a coordinated ring — can be detected without any labels at all.
§2.4
Adom et al. (2022) — SHAP vs LIME comparison

SHAP vs LIME for Explainability

Why the thesis chose SHAP over LIME for explaining loan decisions. Both tell you which features influenced a prediction — but they work differently.

LIME

Speed: Fast ⚡
Consistency: Noisy ✗
Method: Local linear approximation

Perturbs input slightly, fits simple model nearby. Quick but unstable across runs.

VS

SHAP ✓

Speed: Slower
Consistency: Principled ✓
Method: Shapley values (game theory)

Calculates each feature's fair contribution across all orderings. Mathematically defensible.

EU regulators require banks to explain automated credit decisions (GDPR "right to explanation"). SHAP's consistency makes it the defensible choice.

§2.7
Cross-Tier Capital Flows — Literature Synthesis

What Exists vs. What's Missing

Three sources inform the multi-directional lending modules — but none compose into a single four-tier DeFi lending specification.

Project Pine (NY Fed/BIS, 2025)
Hierarchy of smart contracts for central bank operations — factory contracts spawning facility contracts with reserve checks. Validates CWB's layered contract-factory pattern.
Bindseil (2020, ECB)
CBDC interest rates should be tiered — lower for deposits going up, higher for loans going down. Informs CWB's asymmetric rate structure and natural spread.
Dong, Qiu & Xu (2023)
Blockchain cross-tier financing in supply chains — transparent ledger lets capital reach deep-tier participants that traditional delegate financing misses.
⚠ THE GAP: No prior work specifies DeFi lending with reserve-ratio enforcement at all four tiers simultaneously

§2.9
MiCA (EU, Dec 2024) & GENIUS Act (US, Jul 2025)

Stablecoin Regulation

Two laws published after most DeFi protocols were designed fundamentally change the landscape. The thesis reads these as validation, not obstacles.

🇪🇺 EU MiCA Regulation

  • Fiat-pegged tokens = "electronic-money tokens"
  • 1:1 reserve backing required
  • Issuer authorization before public offering
  • Regular audits mandated

🇺🇸 US GENIUS Act

  • First federal payment-stablecoin framework
  • Similar reserve & disclosure obligations

ACM Trans. Web [154]

Web3 KYC innovations survey — zkLogin, zkKYC as leading privacy primitives for FinTech.

Empirical benchmarks: ZKP verification is faster and cheaper than plaintext on-chain KYC; ~70% fewer data fields exposed to verifiers — aligns with EU MiCA privacy-by-design [R36].
🧠 Quick Check: Why two circuits instead of one?

KYC proves who you are (age, eligibility) at onboarding. zkAML proves how your wallet behaves (velocity, sanctions) continuously. Different data, different circuits, same privacy guarantee.
